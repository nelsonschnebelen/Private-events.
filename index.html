The system

Three levers turn a room into a revenue engine.

Every high-performing private-events program does the same three things well. Get one right and you'll book. Get all three right and you'll be turning parties away.

01

Package

Stop selling a blank canvas. Sell a decision. Clear tiers, all-in pricing, and a minimum make "yes" easy.

  • 3 tiers — good / better / signature
  • Per-guest pricing + a booking minimum
  • Named around occasions, not room numbers
  • One obvious "most popular" choice
02

Experience

People don't remember the menu — they remember the moment. Engineer the beats that make guests brag.

  • A signature welcome the second they arrive
  • One dedicated host who owns the night
  • A "wow" beat built for phones & toasts
  • A send-off that earns the referral
03

Convert

Demand exists — you just have to catch it. A tight ad-to-landing-page funnel fills the inquiry inbox.

  • Occasion-targeted ads that stop the scroll
  • A landing page built to answer "does it fit?"
  • A short, high-intent inquiry form
  • A same-day follow-up that closes
Lever 01 — Package

Pick a package, not a puzzle.

The fastest way to kill a booking is to make someone design their own event from scratch. Overwhelmed planners don't reply. Give them three confident choices and a clear minimum, and you move the conversation from "how much would it be?" to "which one fits?"

Anchor with a premium tier, make the middle the obvious pick, and price per guest so the total scales itself.

  • Name tiers for the feeling, not the food — "The Celebration," not "Menu B"
  • Bundle the friction in: space, service, AV, a cake option
  • Publish minimums so unqualified leads self-select out
  • Always have one "most popular" to break decision paralysis

Lever 02 — Experience

Design the moment they'll retell.

A private event is theater. The food has to be great — but greatness is the price of entry. What earns the five-star review and the "who did you use?" text is a sequence of engineered moments that feel effortless to the guest and are anything but to you.

Map the night as beats. Own the arrival, the reveal, and the send-off, and the middle takes care of itself.

  • A named event lead so the host never hunts for someone
  • One signature moment worth filming and posting
  • Small personal touches — the guest of honor's name, in print
  • A graceful close that makes the next booking feel obvious

Lever 03 — Convert

From a scroll-stopping ad to a booked event.

Most restaurants "do private events" with a buried page and a generic email address. That's not a program — it's a hope. A real funnel starts with an occasion-specific ad, sends traffic to a page built for one job, and captures a qualified inquiry while intent is hot.

The landing page's entire purpose is to answer three questions fast: Does it fit my group? Can I picture it? How do I ask?

  • Ads targeted by occasion — holiday parties, milestones, corporate
  • A fit-checker so guests instantly see the right space
  • A short form that asks only what qualifies the lead
  • A Meta Lead event so ad spend optimizes toward bookings
